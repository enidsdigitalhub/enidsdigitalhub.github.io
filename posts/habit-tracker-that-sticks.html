How to use a habit tracker (and actually stick with it)

A habit tracker is one of the simplest tools there is: a grid, a list of habits, and a box to tick each day. Yet most of them are abandoned by the second week. The tracker isn't the problem. The setup usually is. Here's how to set one up so it keeps working.

1. Track fewer habits than you think

It's tempting to list ten things you want to change. Start with three. A page full of empty boxes feels like failure; a page with three mostly-ticked rows feels like progress, and that feeling is what keeps you coming back.

2. Make each habit small enough to do on a bad day

"Exercise for an hour" breaks the first busy day. "Walk for 10 minutes" survives it. Write the smallest version that still counts. You can always do more, but the tick only needs the minimum.

3. Be specific about when

Attach each habit to something you already do: "after morning tea, read 5 pages" or "before bed, plan tomorrow's top 3." A clear trigger beats willpower every time.

4. Keep the tracker where you'll see it

Print it and stick it on the fridge, the mirror or the inside of your planner. If you prefer digital, pin it to your phone's home screen. Out of sight really is out of mind.

5. Never miss twice

You will miss days, and that's normal. The rule that matters is: don't miss two in a row. One blank box is life; two is the start of a new (unwanted) habit.

6. Review once a week

Every Sunday, look at the week. Which habit was easy? Which kept slipping? Make the hard one smaller, or swap it out. A tracker is a tool for learning about yourself, not a test you pass or fail.
